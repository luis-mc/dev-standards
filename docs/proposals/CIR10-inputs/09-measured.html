What the gap is made of. The suites themselves came in 3 minutes over the estimate. The other 8 minutes are hand-overs: the queue before the Linux job and four short status jobs after it that each wait for the one Linux runner. Folding the status jobs into the Linux job (CIO1 as planned) and not running the artifact prune on the Linux runner while a release runs would bring the measured run to about 31 minutes.

Failed rounds cost the most. RELEASE-145 took 13 rounds. Rounds 9 to 12 each failed on one test that a rerun of the failed part alone would have caught in minutes; each round reran every part (owner question 2 Nov 23:05, decision D28). CIR17 and CIR18 (in RELEASE-147) make a push rerun only the parts that failed or whose inputs changed.

Cloud sessions as runners (CIR13 proof, 2 Nov). Ten shards, each in one 4-CPU session, one after another: 92 min in all; the longest shard (e2e 2 of 2) took 12.2 min. Run side by side, the ten would finish in about 12 to 13 minutes, which meets the 15-minute goal without new hardware. The timing probe read 95 to 114 ms against a reference of 82 ms on the PC (scale 1.15 to 1.39), so the budgets were stretched by that much and all passed.
