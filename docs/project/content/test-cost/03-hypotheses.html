Ten hypotheses, each with the test that could have proved it wrong. Three were false or overstated, and say so.

#HypothesisTestVerdict
H1One change is tested many times overCount spec-lint runs per stage in the run historyTrue. 24 full runs in 14 hours for 5 releases. In the 7.1.0 train, 10 agent-branch runs used 132 of 167 CI minutes (79%); the ready PR then repeated all of it.
H2Mutation passes dominate CI timeE2: time every scenario and mutant of the gate suiteTrue. 94 mutants take 81% of the gate suite (1,357 of 1,682 s on Windows), and the gate suite is 62% of a ready-PR run (10.9 of 17.5 min).
H3The run on main after a merge repeats the ready PRCompare the merged tree with the tested tree; read every failed main runTrue. In all three merges checked, the only difference was record files under docs/artifacts/. Its one failure was a flake (git clone could not copy a file), not a bug.
H4The artifacts suite's fake-renderer group repeats the real groupStatic read of every case; E1: time every case in both groups; read every failed runTrue. The real group runs every case but one, and every assertion that differs is weaker in the fake group. Each group costs the same per case (median ratio 1.02), so the fake group is 46% of the suite. It never caught a bug the real group missed.
H4aThe fake group is twice as slow as the real one (my earlier claim)E1, measured directlyFalse. The CI log timestamps I used come from buffered output. Measured: fake 485 s, real 561 s.
H5Agents' local runs wasted this PC's CPUCount local suite executions in 45 transcripts, before and after the 02:30 ruleTrue before 02:30, fixed after. Before the rule: 17 full mutation runs of the gate suite at about 28 min each (E2), about 8 CPU-hours. After it: 1.
H6A join re-runs the suites (my earlier claim)Look for join.py and branch_gate.py runs in this repositoryFalse here. dev-standards has no gate.json; its joins are plain git merges. The gate on join exists only in the templates products get.
H7Record commands cost a lot (each validates and renders every page)Time check, render, pending, set-task-statusFalse. 1.6 to 1.8 s each, 4.0 s for a status change including the push.
H8Record commits flood main and its checksClassify every commit on main and every secret-scan runTrue. 264 of 276 commits on main since 10 October are record commits, and 189 of 194 secret-scan runs on main scanned one. Cheap (10 s each), but they bury the 12 real commits and keep main moving under every CI run.
H9Stopping a mutant at its first failed check would halve the mutation passE2: for each mutant, time to its first failed check against its full runPartly true. 51% of mutant time comes after the first failure, so about 49% is saved, not the audit's 45 to 65%. Uneven: e2e_map, push_check and fast_report fail in the first third; timeout and verify fail late and save little.

What the extra runs did catch. In the window, CI found three Linux-only bugs that Windows runs could not (DS17 twice: a test PATH that dropped /usr/bin; DS29: a missing origin/main case) and one weak test (a DS29 mutant nobody caught). So the checks themselves earn their place. The problem is running them several times, and running all of them whatever changed.
